Original practice questions; not official or leaked. Objectives paraphrase the D6 task statements in Exam Guide v1.0 (see 00_Exam_Scope_and_Study_Plan.md). Multi-response items (Q127, Q137) say "(Select TWO)" and have 5 choices.

Q120D6Intermediate

Objective: Elicit and clarify business requirements through discovery

Scenario

A VP says, "We need an AI agent for customer support." No metrics, volumes, or constraints have been shared.

Question

What should the architect do first?

  1. AStart building a prototype with the most capable model.
  2. BRun discovery: the target workflow, baseline volumes and costs, success metrics, data sources, risk tolerance, and constraints, then confirm the problem is a fit for an LLM solution.
  3. CSelect a vector database.
  4. DWrite the system prompt.
Show answer & explanation

Correct answer B

Explanation

Without a defined problem and measurable success criteria, every downstream choice is a guess.

Why not the others

A, C, and D are solution steps taken before requirements exist.

Architectural principle

Problem and metrics before solution.

Q121D6Advanced

Objective: Elicit and clarify business requirements through discovery

Scenario

Two executives give conflicting goals: Operations wants minimum cost per ticket; Legal wants zero unreviewed customer-facing statements.

Question

What is the best approach?

  1. APick the more senior executive's goal silently.
  2. BBuild to cost and handle Legal later.
  3. CSurface the conflict, translate both goals into measurable constraints, propose options with trade-offs (e.g., draft-only for regulated categories, auto-send for low-risk ones), and get a documented decision.
  4. DPromise both goals will be fully met.
Show answer & explanation

Correct answer C

Explanation

Conflicting requirements are resolved explicitly with options and recorded decisions.

Why not the others

A and B hide the conflict; D overpromises.

Architectural principle

Make trade-offs explicit and decided.

Q122D6Intermediate

Objective: Communicate trade-offs and set realistic expectations

Scenario

A stakeholder asks for "100% accuracy" on policy answers.

Question

What is the best response?

  1. AAgree; the model will be accurate.
  2. BRefuse the project.
  3. CSay accuracy cannot be discussed.
  4. DReframe into measurable targets (e.g., grounded-answer rate on a labeled set, escalation for low confidence), explain residual risk, and show how controls and review manage it.
Show answer & explanation

Correct answer D

Explanation

LLM systems are probabilistic; set measurable targets and managed residual risk rather than absolutes.

Why not the others

A overpromises; B and C don't help the stakeholder decide.

Architectural principle

Replace absolutes with measurable, managed risk.
