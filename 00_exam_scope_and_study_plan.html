Official task statements (verbatim structure from Exam Guide §6)

D1 Solution Design & Architecture: translate business problems into Claude-based solutions · design end-to-end architectures (input → processing → output → feedback loops) · select architectural patterns (workflow, agentic, augmented LLM) · design multi-agent systems and orchestration · apply decomposition techniques · align solutions to business value pillars (efficiency, transformation, productivity, cost, performance SLAs).

D2 Models, Prompting & Context: select Claude models based on trade-offs · design system prompts, templates, guardrails · apply zero-shot / few-shot / chain-of-thought · optimize context windows and token usage · implement prompt reuse (caching, modular prompts, Skills).

D3 Integration: evaluate tool/agent configuration for capability bloat · analyze authN/authZ to find security gaps · evaluate accuracy–latency trade-offs · analyze observability challenges and choose monitoring at scale · design a RAG pipeline (chunking, indexing) · match retrieval strategy to data shape and query pattern · select integration mechanism (MCP, API/CLI, agent-to-agent) · evaluate progressive discovery vs. monolithic context.

D4 Evaluation, Testing & Optimization: define metrics (accuracy, latency, cost, safety, security) · design eval datasets and test frameworks with mixed methodologies · A/B testing and iterative improvement · diagnose issues (prompt failure, hallucination, model mismatch) · optimize tokens/latency/cost-performance · monitor with logging and observability.

D5 Governance, Safety & Risk: guardrails and safety controls · risks, limitations, failure modes · human-in-the-loop validation · compliance (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA, FedRAMP) · ethical AI (bias, fairness, transparency).

D6 Stakeholder Communication & Lifecycle: structured discovery and requirements · communicate decisions and trade-offs · feedback loops and expectation alignment (incl. SLAs) · document architectures and give implementation guidance · lifecycle phases (discovery, design, handoff, monitoring, iteration).

D7 Developer Productivity & Operational Enablement: configure Claude tools/environments for teams (e.g., Claude Code) · improve developer workflows with AI-assisted tooling · support debugging and operational issue resolution.

3. Important discrepancy: your five areas vs. the exam's seven domains

Your prompt lists five areas. Those match the five lessons of the official prep course, not the exam blueprint:

Prep-course lesson (official; duration) Maps to exam domains (my mapping from titles/descriptions — inferred, not stated by Anthropic)
1. Claude Platform & Solution Design (238 min) D1 + D2
2. Enterprise Integration & Production (158 min) D3 + part of D4 (budgets, observability, eval gates)
3. Responsible AI, Safety & Risk for Architects (114 min) D5
4. Stakeholder Engagement, Lifecycle & GTM (178 min) D6
5. Team Enablement & Operational Productivity (45 min) D7

Gaps to know about 1. Evaluation (D4, 16%) has no dedicated prep lesson. The course objective "build evaluations as acceptance criteria… the gate before any model or architecture change" touches it, but you must study it as its own domain. This guide does. 2. Prep-course prerequisites (recommended, not required): Claude 101, Claude Code in Action, AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations, Building with the Claude API, Introduction to Model Context Protocol, AI Capabilities and Limitations. 3. RAG, compliance regimes (GDPR/HIPAA/FedRAMP), and the stakeholder domain are explicitly in the blueprint; make sure you cover them even though some prep lessons are brief. 4. Go-to-market appears in the prep-course title but the Exam Guide's D6 task statements don't mention it explicitly. Treat GTM/positioning as supplemental.

How this guide is organized: by the 7 official domains (Parts 1–7), each tagged with the prep-course lesson it supports. Questions follow the official weights (see Part 9).

5. How the exam rewards thinking (from the three official samples)

All three official samples share a trap structure worth internalizing:

  • Sample 1 (least privilege): remove unneeded capability (B) beats monitoring it (logging) or compensating for it (confirmation prompts), and a bigger model doesn't change authorization scope.
  • Sample 2 (latency + cost): put static content first and enable prompt caching (C); don't truncate required policy, don't downsize blindly, don't move it to few-shot.
  • Sample 3 (confident wrong answers after document refresh): investigate retrieval/indexing first (B) — it's what changed.

Pattern: find the root cause or the structural control; reject compensating-control theater. Expect distractors that are plausible, responsible-sounding, and wrong for the specific problem.
